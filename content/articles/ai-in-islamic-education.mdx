Artificial intelligence has moved from novelty to infrastructure in most classrooms. For teachers of
Qur'anic and Arabic Studies, the question is no longer whether to engage with these tools, but where to
draw firm boundaries around them.

## Where AI genuinely helps

Used well, AI tools can support lesson planning, generate practice exercises for Nahw and Sarf, and help
learners revise vocabulary at their own pace. They are strongest where the task is mechanical: repetition,
formatting and first drafts that a teacher then reviews.

## Where the boundary sits

Tafsir, hadith authentication and fatwa are matters of scholarly chain and accountability. No automated
system should stand in for a qualified teacher on questions of religious ruling. AI can prepare supporting
materials; it cannot carry isnad.

## A working framework

A simple rule has served well in practice: AI drafts, the teacher verifies, and the student is always told
which parts were machine assisted. That transparency protects both the integrity of the subject and the
trust between teacher and student.
